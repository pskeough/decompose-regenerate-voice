Cross-cutting findings from the gates:

- Every structural and lexical measure separates the author from the 4B base on at least 8 of 9 authors at d >= 0.5 (opener classes, paragraph CV, hapax, MATTR, MTLD, corrected burstiness). The manipulation check is not the hard gate; the replicate floor is.
- Nothing correlates with LUAR above 0.33. The layered set measures things the primary does not, which is the design intent and matches Sawant's |r| < 0.07 across traditions.
- The thin-data adapters (cather 33 briefs, sui_sin_far 25, h_private 38 within-work) fail every structural measure in the wrong direction (opener distance up, paragraph CV not recovered, echo rate 4-7x, terminal event missing on 58-61% of cells). The data floor from the size curve (51-100 briefs) is now visible on five independent instruments.
- The adapter's coherence cost is real and specific: proposition echo rises at 8B and 14B on both authors (chesterton 0.030 -> 0.078 at 14B), and the last brief event goes missing more often; opener monotony, paragraph shape and figure rates improve at the same time. Style and construction move in opposite directions under LoRA, which is the paper's framing for the human study.
- Length: only the uncapped echo rate, rep_4 and distinct_3 exceed |rho| 0.4 with word count; every reported measure is either paired to the source, windowed, or capped.

Per-run picture (both judges, every size): base > adapter on the construction block by 1.5 to 3 points and on the total by 1 to 2.5; both blocks rise monotonically with base size (patrick_v2 totals 3.40 / 4.48 / 5.39 / 6.40 at 1.7B / 4B / 8B / 14B on Gemini); adapters rise too but stay below the base at the same size (2.40 / 3.16 / 3.75 / 4.23). The only subscore adapters win is S1 opening move.

Verdict: as a CONSTRUCTION instrument the rubric passes (shuffle test, kappa 0.5-0.6 on C2/C5, agreement with PER, item-1 B last) and is adopted at factor level beside PER. As a VOICE or quality criterion it fails on the private author: it ranks the 14B bare model above his own prose and above the cell he chose blind. That is the Tutone et al. judge-prefers-machine bias measured on our data, and it is the argument for the author-owned validation set as the criterion and for reporting judge scores only relative to the author's own passages scored blind in the same batch.
