# Decompose and regenerate: authorial voice in small open-weight models

Small open-weight models can now be adapted to a single writer on a consumer GPU, yet the field has few controlled measurements of what that adaptation buys in voice and what it costs in following the writer's instructions. This repository holds a decompose-and-regenerate study of that question. Each held-out passage by an author is reduced to a content-only brief by an API model from a different family, the brief passes leakage gates, and the passage is regenerated by Qwen3 at 4B, 8B and 14B and by Ministral 3 at 3B, 8B and 14B. Generation runs under six prompting arms (brief only, author name, three retrieved exemplars, a style guide, a quantitative style block, and all of them together) and with a per-author LoRA adapter trained on brief-to-passage pairs.

Across 13 authors (6 period public-domain, 5 contemporary under Creative Commons licences, 2 private corpora) the study holds 110 main runs and 40,726 generated passages, with three hosted models as comparators. Outputs are scored with three style embeddings (LUAR as the pre-registered primary, Wegmann, StyleDistance), stylometry, construction and degeneracy measures, and two LLM judges for brief coverage and two-alternative forced choice. Every contrast is read against a replicate floor, the largest difference between two generations of the same run.

## Results

On the embeddings the adapter moves the model toward the author at every size, with LUAR cosine to the source rising by 0.049 at 4B, 0.066 at 8B and 0.046 at 14B against a floor of 0.023. Attribution to the target author rises from an author mean of 0.33 to 0.60 at 4B, against a chance rate of 0.07, and the 8-gram overlap with the training text never exceeds 0.01. In exchange the adapter loses 27 to 34 points of judged brief coverage, and no author escapes that loss. Truncation and proposition echo rise and vocabulary narrows.

Parameter count does not change the adapter gain, which moves by -0.002 LUAR per doubling, while base coverage rises 6.4 points per doubling. Corpus size is the moderator that survives. With more training briefs the gain grows (Spearman 0.72 for LUAR, 0.86 for coverage), and the adherence cost flattens between about 50 and 150 briefs. A within-author series on Chesterton shows the same curve, with a LUAR gain of 0.022 at 25 briefs, 0.085 at 50 and 0.119 at 235.

Prompting buys little. The author's name and retrieved exemplars give about sixty percent of the 4B adapter's LUAR gain at a fifth of its adherence cost, and the style guide and numeric style block are inert or harmful, with the numeric block lowering LUAR by 0.047 at 14B. Against the hosted models the 4B adapter ties on the embeddings once the corpus passes about 100 briefs and loses below 50. Hosted models keep 44 to 46 more coverage points than the adapter, and their truncation rate is zero against 11 percent for the adapter. All three hosted models sit within a floor of one another on every voice instrument. Ministral replicates the direction of every effect on three authors but not its size, and one private author stays invisible to the embeddings in both families. We read the adapter as a corpus-dependent move toward the author's register bought at a steady price in adherence. Because the instruments and the judges disagree so often, these data support no single fidelity number.

## Design

| factor | levels |
|---|---|
| generator | Qwen3 4B, 8B, 14B (1.7B for one author); Ministral 3 3B, 8B, 14B (3 authors) |
| conditioning | A0 brief only, A1 + author name, A2 + 3 BM25-retrieved exemplars, A3 + style guide, A4 + quantitative style block, A5 all of A1-A4 |
| adapter | LoRA r=16, alpha=32, all linear layers, trained on brief-to-passage pairs (6 epochs), assistant tokens only |
| comparators | deepseek-v4-flash (F0, F5), gpt-5.6-luna (F0), deepseek-v4-pro (F0) |
| decomposer | deepseek-v4-flash at temperature 0, with leakage gates (no shared 6-gram, at most 3% shared 4-grams, brief at most 35% of source length, no style vocabulary) |
| authors | period: Wharton, Cather, Chesterton, Chopin, Mansfield, Sui Sin Far; contemporary: Doctorow, Watts, Link, Stross, Rucker; private: two unpublished corpora |
| samples | two per cell, fixed seeds; replicate runs at every size in both families |
| size series | Chesterton 25-235 briefs, Doctorow 25-525, one private author 25-75, each with a second seed at 50 |

The pre-registered protocol, its amendments and the threats to validity are in `docs/METHODS.md`; every prompt is in `docs/PROMPTS.md`; references in `docs/CITATIONS.md`.

## Reproducing

The analysis scripts resolve paths from the repository root and write into `results/`. Scripts 10, 11, 12, 14, 15, 17, 20, 22, 25, 26, 27, 29 and 30 run from `data/master/` and the shipped results folders. Scripts 01, 13, 16, 18, 19, 21, 23, 24 and 28 also read the per-run archive (generations for every author, embeddings, judge records; about 4.5 GB, not included). Dependencies: Python 3.12 or later, pandas, numpy, scipy, statsmodels, matplotlib, pyarrow.

```bash
python code/analysis/run_all.py --only 10,11,25
```

Generation for Qwen3 4B ran on llama.cpp on a 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti; the 8B and 14B rungs, the Ministral family and the size series ran on rented L40S and A6000 GPUs.
